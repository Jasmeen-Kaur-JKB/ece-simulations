DC motor: force, torque and the commutator

A coil carries current in a magnetic field. The two sides carry opposite currents, so they feel opposite forces. Switch the commutator on and off to see why it is needed.

What to notice

  1. A current-carrying conductor in a field feels a force F = B I L.
  2. The two coil sides carry opposite currents, so one force points up and the other down. Together they make a torque.
  3. When the coil is square-on to the field (the neutral plane), the forces point along the coil and the torque is zero.
  4. Past that point, a fixed current would reverse the torque. The commutator reverses the current at the neutral plane, so the torque keeps its direction.
